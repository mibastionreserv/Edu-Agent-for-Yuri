## Why metrics
Scrum is empirical, so teams need transparent metrics to forecast and to improve — decisions are based on observed data, not guesses.

## Velocity
Velocity is the amount of work (in story points, units of relative complexity) a team completes in a Sprint. It helps the Product Owner forecast releases from history rather than hope. Velocity is unique to each team; use the trend or average, never a single Sprint, and never compare teams by it.

## Burndown chart
A Burndown Chart shows how much work remains across the days of the Sprint — the key inspection tool at the Daily Scrum. An ideal line runs from the starting total to zero; the actual line shows reality. If the actual line sits above the ideal, the team is behind; scope added mid-Sprint makes the line jump up.

## Lead time and cycle time
Lead Time and Cycle Time are flow (Lean/Kanban) metrics. Lead Time is from an idea entering the backlog to release. Cycle Time is from the moment a Developer starts active work on an item to when it is Done. Shorter, more predictable times mean smoother flow.

## The golden rule of metrics
Metrics exist for the team's own inspection and adaptation. Used as management targets they get gamed and stop telling the truth.

## Our velocity dropped — is the team lazy?
Velocity isn't a productivity score. One low Sprint means little; maybe items were bigger, someone was on leave, or an impediment struck. Look at the trend over five or six Sprints, not one dot. Never use velocity to judge people — that's the fastest way to get inflated estimates and dishonest data. Bring the dip to the Retrospective as a question, not an accusation.

## Difference between lead time and cycle time
Lead time is the full trip — idea in the backlog to released. Cycle time is just the working part — from when a Developer starts it to Done. A big gap between them means work waits in a queue before anyone touches it: a flow problem, not a coding problem.
