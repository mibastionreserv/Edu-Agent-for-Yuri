## Artifacts and commitments
Artifacts represent work or value and are designed to maximise transparency. Each artifact has a commitment that gives it focus and lets progress be measured.

## Product Backlog
The Product Backlog is an emergent, ordered list of everything that might be needed in the product; the single source of work for the team. The Product Owner owns it. Its commitment is the Product Goal, a long-term objective the team plans toward.

## Sprint Backlog
The Sprint Backlog is the Sprint Goal (why), the set of items selected for the Sprint (what), and the plan to deliver them (how). It is by and for the Developers, a highly visible, real-time picture of the Sprint's work. Its commitment is the Sprint Goal.

## Increment
An Increment is a concrete, usable stepping stone toward the Product Goal. Each Increment is additive to prior ones and thoroughly verified. Multiple Increments may be created in a Sprint. Its commitment is the Definition of Done.

## Definition of Done
The Definition of Done is a formal, shared description of the quality state work must reach to be part of an Increment. When an item meets the Definition of Done, an Increment is born. If it doesn't meet it, it cannot be released or presented as Done at the Sprint Review — it returns to the Product Backlog.

## Who writes the Definition of Done?
The Scrum Team owns it. If your organisation has a shared standard, you start from that and can make it stricter, never looser. The point is one honest, shared meaning of 'Done' that everyone trusts.

## Refinement
Refinement is the ongoing activity of adding detail, estimates, and order to Product Backlog items so they're ready for a future Sprint. Items nearer the top are clearer and smaller.

## An item is 90% done at Sprint end — what happens?
There is no '90%' in Scrum. It is not part of the Increment and returns to the Product Backlog. Done or not Done.
