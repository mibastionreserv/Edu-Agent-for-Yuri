## What Scrum is
Scrum is a lightweight framework that helps people and teams deliver value through adaptive solutions to complex problems. It is intentionally incomplete — it defines only the parts needed to make empiricism work. It is a framework, not a fixed step-by-step methodology.

## Empiricism and lean thinking
Scrum is founded on empiricism: knowledge comes from experience and decisions are based on what is observed. It also uses lean thinking: reduce waste and focus on the essentials. The three empirical pillars are transparency, inspection, and adaptation.

## The one Scrum Team
A Scrum Team is one cohesive unit of typically ten or fewer people, with no sub-teams or hierarchies. It is cross-functional (together it has every skill needed) and self-managing (it decides how to do the work), focused on one Product Goal.

## The three accountabilities
There are three accountabilities: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. They are peers, not a management hierarchy.

## Product Owner
The Product Owner is accountable for maximising the value of the product. They are the sole owner of the Product Backlog, represent the business and the users, and own the What and the Why. It is one person, not a committee, and their decisions are respected.

## Scrum Master
The Scrum Master is accountable for the team's effectiveness and for establishing Scrum. They are a true leader who serves — a coach and facilitator, not a project manager or a boss. They remove impediments and own how we work.

## Developers
The Developers are accountable for creating a usable Increment each Sprint. They are a cross-functional, self-organising group (design, code, test, and more) and own how to build it.

## The Scrum Values
The five Scrum Values are Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, and Courage. Living the values builds the trust that makes empiricism work.

## Is the Scrum Master the team's manager?
No. The Scrum Master is not a manager and gives no orders. They serve the team: coaching good habits, facilitating the events, and removing impediments. Authority over what to build sits with the Product Owner; how to build sits with the Developers.

## Can one person be both Product Owner and Scrum Master?
Scrum does not forbid it, but it is risky. The roles pull in different directions — the Product Owner pushes for value and scope, the Scrum Master protects the team's health and process. One person wearing both hats tends to shortchange one of them, so keep them separate when you can.
