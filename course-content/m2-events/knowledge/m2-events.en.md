## The Sprint
The Sprint is the heartbeat of Scrum and the container for all other events. It is a fixed length of one month or less — most often two weeks — and a new Sprint starts immediately after the previous one ends.

## A Sprint's length is fixed
A Sprint's length is fixed; it cannot be extended or shortened once started. Scope may be clarified and renegotiated with the Product Owner, but the Sprint Goal and quality are not sacrificed. Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint.

## Sprint Planning
Sprint Planning starts the Sprint. It answers Why (the Sprint Goal), What (which Product Backlog items are selected), and How (a plan to deliver them). Its outputs are the Sprint Goal and the Sprint Backlog. Time-box: up to eight hours for a one-month Sprint, proportionally less for shorter Sprints (about four hours for two weeks).

## Daily Scrum
The Daily Scrum is a fifteen-minute event for the Developers, held at the same time and place every working day. Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the plan for the next day. It is not a status report to a manager.

## Sprint Review
The Sprint Review happens near the end of the Sprint. The Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the Increment and what changed, and collaborate on what to do next; the Product Backlog is adapted. It is a working session, not merely a demo. Time-box: up to four hours for a one-month Sprint.

## Sprint Retrospective
The Sprint Retrospective concludes the Sprint. The team inspects how the last Sprint went — individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done — and plans concrete improvements. Time-box: up to three hours for a one-month Sprint.

## What if work isn't finished at the end of a Sprint?
The Sprint length is fixed and must never be extended. If an item isn't finished it simply isn't part of the Increment. It moves back into the Product Backlog, and at the next Sprint Planning the Product Owner decides whether it is still worth doing.

## Can stakeholders talk during the Daily Scrum?
The Daily Scrum belongs to the Developers — it is their fifteen minutes to re-plan the day toward the Sprint Goal. Others may observe, but it is not a status meeting for stakeholders or managers. Save that conversation for the Sprint Review, where stakeholders are exactly who we want in the room.
